    Questions

    Asked, answered, on the record.

    What is an AI organism?+

    A software system designed like a living thing: a cortex that reasons, memory that persists across every session, cells that do the work, an immune system that watches for drift — and a proof loop that publishes evidence of all of it. It grows by integrating what works, never by quietly rewriting what it was.

    Where can I inspect proof?+

    Every organism ships a public ledger — build artifacts, status labels, and dated transmissions. Inspect the proof ledger and read all dispatches in the transmissions archive. The rule is simple: if a capability isn't in the ledger, treat it as not real.

    Can an organism move money or publish on its own?+

    No. Money motions and outward publications sit behind hard human-approval gates enforced in the architecture, not in a prompt. Zero hidden authority paths is a design invariant — it's one of the three numbers we publish.

    What happens when an organism fails?+

    It fails in public. Incidents go into the same ledger as wins, and the organism metabolizes the failure into new structure — a perception gate, a confirmation rule, an immune response. Failure is food.
