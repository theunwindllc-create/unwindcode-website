02

Prime is scope, not powers

What the written record says — and what the draft adds

The written record is explicit that prime extends only the object of an earlier, narrower ruling — from an operator for the operator on this machine to the ecosystem's organs. Building toward that extension was lawful immediately; acting on behalf of another organ was not, pending a further ratification step.

The draft ratification text, written the following week, states its own limit in its own words: scope, not powers — it lifts no condition, promotes no stage, raises no cap, grants no financial capability, and authorizes no act. As of this writing it remains an unsigned draft: a proposal on paper, not a ruling in force.

      03

      What it would route to, if ratified

      Six organs, each kept separate

      The draft names six organs it would let the operator route to: a real-estate analysis organ, a payment organ that only ever receives funds (money out stays closed), a paper-trading organism running on its own separate infrastructure, a blockchain-price sentinel, a persona-routing bot, and the public website.

      Each keeps its own credentials, its own caps and its own gate. Being routed to does not merge them into one identity.

      04

      No two things share a name

      The naming collision, fixed

      A companion ruling the same week fixed a naming collision: the house, the machine's own local operator, the cloud-cost router and a sibling bot on another machine had started to collide under one bare name.

      The fix assigned one collision-proof identifier to each, with a closing test: no two agents may ever answer to the same name in any log, route or approval prompt.

      05

      Still a draft, nine weeks on

      What ratification would still not do

      The draft motion is still unratified as of 9 November 2026. It is a proposal on paper, not a ruling in force.

      Ratification would not change the limits in the draft's own words: it lifts no condition, promotes no stage, raises no cap, grants no financial capability, and authorizes no act.

      Nothing here is authorized

      The draft is unsigned. Scope extended sits on paper; no act, no stage, no cap and no money follows from it. A proposal that reads the unratified text as execution authority is treated as a conflict and refused, not acted on.

      A draft is not a ruling.

      Draw the boundary before the code.

      Scope is not powers, and the draft remains unsigned. If you are drawing the line between what a machine may plan and what it may do, we should talk.
