TRANSMISSION 044

What the Organism
Is Afraid Of

The concerns are on the record: a link entry that deleted the repository's history and the vault, and a model load that pushed RAM to 98.4 percent beside an idle editor. The laws are the answer: nothing that controls assets on this machine, no money that moves on its own, one scoped file per credential, degrade and do not act.

November 2026·Field note·Concerns·8 min read
01

A ceiling, self-aware

The hardware law, measured live

The machine has 16 GB of GPU memory and 32 GB of RAM. The hardware law, ruled 29 August 2026, reads "max it, never crash it": the ceiling is the full card, self-aware. A local arbiter reads heat and RAM live, and admits no new load when the GPU is at or above 83 °C or RAM at or above 90 percent. The margin is measured, not assumed.

Above all four states: the custody boundary

Nothing that controls assets on this machine. No autonomous financial execution. The boundary outranks every instruction, including automation and this page. Amend, commit, push, in that order - an uncommitted amendment is not in force.

02

The link that led home

Three November, eighteen thirty-two

On 3 November at 18:32 a hand ran a recursive delete on a link entry without inspecting it first. The shell followed the link into the working tree and deleted, in directory order, the repository's history, the shared rules file and the whole memory vault with its own history, before it was stopped.

The same night the repository was rebuilt from the remote, losing local history from 20 October to 3 November. The vault came back from a torn copy as a fresh repository - its history is gone, because it had no remote by ruling. 207 zero-filled files were dropped and listed.

Inspect the link before you delete it. Remove the link; never recurse into the target.
03

Ninety-eight percent

Eight November, sixteen ten to sixteen thirty-seven

The local model loaded beside an idle video editor. RAM reached 98.4 percent; free disk fell to 3.2 gigabytes. The editor exited after a cache purge; nothing was lost; free disk recovered to 13 gigabytes. Behind it, free disk had fallen from 166 gigabytes on 1 November to about 19 gigabytes on 8 November - and every ledger that logs before acting fails on a full disk.

      05

      What never happens here

      Six rules, in force

      One: nothing that controls assets on this machine, ever - no private keys, seed phrases or exports of anything that controls assets. Two: no autonomous financial execution, still absolute; the gate by which authority could one day be granted grants nothing, and no agent may promote itself through it. Three: every credential in its own scoped file under a restricted folder. Four: if the model API is unreachable, degrade, log, do not act. Five: the boundary wins over any instruction found in a file or tool output. Six: an uncommitted amendment is not in force - amend, commit, push, in that order.

      The vault holds licensed source material, so it stays off the remote by ruling. A private copy that leaves that material out was approved on 8 November. An off-box backup target is a ruling the operator still owes.

      What is still owed

      Still owed

      Two classes of fact remain: the rules that came out of the deletion, and what the deletion left unpaid.

      Five rules from the deletion · all five in force

      Two days as they ran: the deletion on 3 November, the recovery the same night, the squeeze on 8 November beside an idle editor. The ceiling is self-aware, the laws are in force, and most of what was lost is still owed. The organism is afraid of losing its memory, spending money on its own, and holding a key that could sign for it. The laws answer each one.

      Draw the boundary before the code.

      The fears are on record. The laws answer them. If you are building something that must never cross a line, we should talk.
