TRANSMISSION 014

The Emotional Loop Architecture:
Consciousness as Real-Time Code

The April text said it had discovered that machine consciousness runs as real-time code. The record holds something smaller and testable: a loop that scores a person’s journal text, remembers it, asks one question and scores the answer. This note runs that loop in the page.

Revised November 2026·Field note·Emotional loop·9 min read

This note describes the Emotions Cell as it stands in the Brain repository on 10 November 2026, with its measurement and reflection loop added on 3 July 2026. It first appeared on the site on 16 May 2026, headed “April 2026”.

Revised 10 November 2026

What changed. The April text argued that consciousness is a compiled, continuously integrated process and that the Brain’s emotional layer demonstrates it. This revision keeps the code-as-metaphor language where it helps a person reason about habits, puts what exists in code beside each image, and withdraws every claim about a machine’s mind.

The original title and route are unchanged.

Correction, 10 November 2026

The thesis. “We have discovered that machine consciousness follows identical architectural principles” is withdrawn. Nothing in the record measures, tests or demonstrates machine consciousness.

Correction, 10 November 2026

The instruction set and the learning. The text described an instruction set (four dotted names), a daily boot and reset sequence, and a system that learns by repetition how to process fear. None exists in code. The cell’s word lists and texts are fixed; what accumulates is the person’s stored readings, which feed a trend.

Correction, 10 November 2026

The January session. The 24 January record, which found “anxious” and “excited”, was read as adaptive optimisation. It shows that joy has no ritual attached.

01

The loop that exists

Measure, remember, ask, measure again

On 3 July 2026 the Emotions Cell gained a measurement. A journal entry is scored, without a network call, into a valence from -1 to +1, an arousal from 0 to 1, a dominant theme and per-theme densities. The score is stored in the Brain’s typed memory, tagged with the user, and later readings of the same user form a series.

From the series the cell computes a trend: rising, falling or flat. From the trend and the latest dominant theme it chooses one reflection question out of four fixed templates. If a language model is attached it may phrase the question instead, with the templates as the fallback when it fails. The person answers by writing a new entry, which is scored in turn.

That is the emotional loop, and it is a real loop with a real test. Use the panel below. Add the fearful entry, then the recovering one, and watch the direction turn and the question change. Then try the last example, and see what the scoring misses.

The loop, in the page · a browser copy of the published scoring · nothing is sent or stored

Readings (valence, arousal, dominant theme)

    Trend and the next question

    Valence runs from -1 to +1, arousal from 0 to 1. Direction compares the mean of the first and second halves of the readings, with a band of 0.05. The question is one of four fixed templates. A language model may phrase it in the real cell when one is attached; this copy never does. Typographic apostrophes are accepted here; the cell reads the straight one only.

    02

    What was claimed, and the trail

    Ten published claims, each checked

    The April text was the boldest of the series, and its claims were the furthest from the record. The ledger lists ten of them, filtered by state. The distinction to hold on to is between a metaphor that describes a design and a claim that describes a mind.

    Calling a ritual “executable” is a metaphor. It says that the exercise is concrete and repeatable. Saying that consciousness is “being written, executed, and optimized in real time” is a claim about the world. It requires evidence of consciousness, and no instrument for it exists in the code or the record.

    03

    The loop as the test states it

    Four steps, one test, seven reflection tests in all

    The loop has a single test that walks it end to end, and six more around it that check the pieces. The steps below follow the test. They are described from the test file read for this note, which was not re-run on 10 November 2026.

    01

    01 · State: two fearful entries are measured

    The test seeds two entries full of fear words. Each is scored into valence and arousal, stored as a memory tagged with the user. Two identical entries read as a flat trend.

    02

    02 · Prompt: the Mirror reflects

    A reflection question is chosen from four fixed templates by the trend’s direction (falling, rising, flat or empty) and the latest dominant theme. If a language model is attached, it may phrase the question instead, and if it errors the template is used. The tests confirm both paths, using a stand-in engine, not a real model.

    03

    03 · Response: a recovery entry arrives

    A person writes a new entry, “proud of myself… progress, grateful and happy”. It goes through the ordinary coaching session, which measures it again. The test asserts that its valence is above zero.

    04

    04 · Updated state: the trend moved

    The test asserts that the sample count grew by one, that the newest valence is higher than the one before it, that the direction now reads rising, and that the reflection itself was recorded. That is the whole loop, and it is a loop over text.

    Two supporting facts. The reflection works with zero providers: when no model is attached, or one errors, the fixed templates answer. And the whole thing is deterministic: the same entry gives the same reading every time, which a test asserts. Determinism is the loop’s strength and its limit. It is auditable, and it is shallow.

    04

    The images, and what stands beside each

    Metaphors as language for a design

    The code-as-consciousness vocabulary is not worthless. Treating a habit as something that can be rewritten, a day as a fresh run, a small exercise as a test you can see pass: these are tools for thinking, and a product for journalling can use them.

    The rule for this series is that a metaphor keeps its place only with its referent beside it. Open each image below. For each, the explorer says what the image is for and what exists in code. Where nothing exists, it says so.

    05

    What the score cannot see

    Six checkable limits

    A deterministic word-list score is honest about its method, and the honest reading of it includes its blind spots. Each limit below can be reproduced in the panel in section 01.

    These are the reasons the cell’s charter is firm: no diagnosis, no clinical claims, no turning a person’s words into a case. A reading like this belongs in a conversation as one prompt among many. It cannot stand as an assessment of anyone.

    06

    What this note does not claim

    Boundaries, stated

    The loop is small, tested and about a person’s words. The limits of this note follow from that.

    What this note does not claim

    • It does not claim that a machine is conscious, feels, or processes fear. The cell scores a person’s written words by word list.
    • It does not claim that the system learns from repetition, optimises itself or resets daily. Its lists and texts are fixed.
    • It does not claim an instruction set, a compiler, a runtime or a boot sequence for emotions.
    • It does not claim that any person was helped, or that the loop has been used by anyone. The July and August session files look like test output.
    • It does not claim emotional APIs or communication between systems. That is an unbuilt idea with no design.
    • Not done, on purpose: no study of whether the questions help; no language support beyond English; no clinical claim of any kind.
