TRANSMISSION 015

How We Give Machines
a Mind

Most software is born finished. We build organisms — systems designed to observe, reason, remember, coordinate, and evolve. This is the architecture behind them.

May 2026·Architecture·Revised 10 Nov 2026·7 min read

Written in May 2026 as a design note. State of the record checked on 10 November 2026 against the consolidation audit of 8 November and the organ-health diagnosis of 10 November.

Revised 10 November 2026. This page began as a design essay and read in places like a status report. It now says which is which.

Correction, 10 November 2026. The two charts that stood in section 03 plotted figures that were never measured: a "capability index" for five domains, and a thirty-cycle series of knowledge nodes and rule mutations that the page generated at random in the browser. Both are removed and replaced by a table of what the record shows. Claims that held for the design but not for the running system, such as nightly reflective logs, a fixed context size, an approval path through a messaging app and a continuously self-auditing mirror, are corrected or removed. Source paths are removed from the module list.

01

Birth — Neurogenesis

The Six-Phase Build Sequence

A new mind does not begin with a prompt. It begins with a design problem. What must this organism notice? Which decisions should it make alone? What kind of failure is unacceptable?

01

Research

Map the terrain, the stakes, and the failure modes. Understand the domain before writing a single line of architecture.

02

Architecture

Define the agent roles, the decision graph, and the control surface. This is the cortex blueprint.

03

Memory Design

Decide what should persist, decay, or be promoted. Four memory layers, set out in section 04.

04

Protocols

Govern how agents communicate, escalate, and recover. The nervous system of orchestration.

05

Knowledge Integration

Load frameworks, references, and domain context the organism needs to reason about its environment.

06

Delivery

Place the organism in a real operating environment where it can run and be observed. Improving by itself is a design aim, not a measured result: section 03 shows what the record holds.

The point is not to imitate a human mind theatrically. The point is to construct a system with enough internal structure that intelligence has somewhere to live. Without that structure, you do not have a mind. You have autocomplete with better branding.

Memory Architecture

Memory is the boundary between a session and a system. Once an organism can distinguish what is transient, reusable, factual, and experiential, it operates with continuity.

05

What We Are Actually Building

Closing Transmission

Unwind is designing systems that are meant to do more than execute commands: to accumulate judgment, to coordinate across specialized parts, and to keep context alive long enough for intelligence to deepen. Section 03 shows how far the record has got.

That is the shift we care about most. Not software that looks intelligent for a moment, but architecture that would let intelligence mature over time.

This is how we give machines a mind.

06

What This Note Does Not Claim

Added 10 November 2026
  • It does not claim that any machine here is conscious, feels, or has a mind. "Mind", "organism", "neural" and "immune system" are language for a design.
  • It does not claim that the modules above are running together as one system. Some are code that has not run for weeks; some are concepts.
  • It does not claim measured capability. The old capability scores were not measurements and are gone.
  • It does not claim self-improvement. On the record, procedures and code are changed by hands and committed; the loop meant to do that on its own has run nothing in forty days.
  • It does not claim to be a current status page. For the day-to-day state, read the newer field notes from 8 November onward.

Build the organism, not the wrapper.
