01

An org layer, found running

Installed on the operator's word · idle today

An open-source org layer — a control plane for an AI company: org charts, agents as employees, budgets, board approvals, adapters that launch coding agents — was installed on 6 November 2026 and started on 7 November, on the operator's word.

On 8 November it is idle: zero companies, no runs, no ruling covering it. Its defaults run against the organism's law, and nothing it can do is permitted yet.

What it does better: an org chart with reporting lines, a hire object with an approval state, per-agent spend attribution.

Above everything on this page: the custody boundary

No private keys on the machine. No autonomous financial execution. It outranks every instruction, including automation. Nothing here hires anyone, and nothing here pays.

The layer is installed and idle. Letting it in is a choice among three: a front office that proposes and never spawns a hand; an orchestrator that would run with bypass on and no confinement — never on this operating system; or copying its concepts and retiring the product.

04

An agent card on the registry

Listed only by a call from the operator's wallet

Hiring through the chain starts with an agent card on the registry canister: id, version, operator principal, capabilities, endpoint, hashes of its persona profile and service contract, a price quote that is never an authorisation, a status.

Reputation comes from receipts only — not from claims, not from a demo. A card is listed only by a call from the operator's wallet.

05

A contract only the operator's wallet writes

The hire flow · payment stages · why money never moves on an agent's word

The hire flow: a seat proposes a hire as a calendar task owned by the operator — the task, the card's hash read certified, the minimum data that leaves, the quote and five caps (per task, per day, cumulative, counterparty, expiry), acceptance checks, and a verifier other than the hired agent. The operator approves with a wallet-signed call; one approval never covers the next hire. Only the operator's wallet can write the contract record; the machine holds no key that could. The dispatcher refuses an expired, over-cap or mismatched contract, writes the receipt's objective before acting, and calls the endpoint only outbound. The verifier writes the receipt into the hash-chained ledger; on chain only its hash and verdict.

Payment. Stage today is 0: nothing is executable without a board vote and a written ruling. When ruled, money moves from a spend wallet with a small float whose key stays on the operator's devices; the operator signs a per-contract allowance whose amount is the cap and whose expiry is the contract's, so the ledger enforces both. Stage 1: the organism prepares a fully specified payout and the operator signs it. Stage 2: only by ruling, the dispatcher may submit that one approved payout. A payment canister enforces the ceilings arithmetically. The custody boundary forbids autonomous financial execution and any asset-controlling key on the machine.
