TRANSMISSION 006

Neurogenesis: The Capacity
for Self-Regeneration

An April 2026 note said a failing organism spins up a lobe, rebuilds a cell, tests it to full coverage and grafts it in unseen. Checked against the record, a pipeline of propose, sandbox, critic and approve exists and is tested; exactly one cell has been grafted; and nothing here heals itself without the operator.

November 2026·Revised note·Evolution·10 min read
Dated. This note describes the evolution engine and its records as they stand on 10 November 2026. The note it replaces was published in April 2026 and described an automatic design in the present tense.

Revised 10 November 2026

Rewritten to the Transmission standard. Ten claims were checked: one supported, four softened, four withdrawn and one kept as a metaphor. The page now describes the pipeline that exists, stage by stage, and the near-failures that shaped it.

Correction, 10 November 2026

The April note said an observer maps the stack trace against a decision log, spins up a refactoring lobe, validates the cell against an agent rules directory, requires one hundred percent coverage and then grafts the cell into the live organism. The record shows a gap analysis from the failure request, a generator and a separate critic, a sandbox that passes on a clean exit, a review-only packet, and a graft only after the operator approves. That rules directory does not exist, coverage is not measured, and no cell grafts itself. Those sentences are withdrawn or softened.

01

Ten claims from April, checked

A ledger of what the old note said

The April note made a dramatic promise. When an agent hits a persistent failure while running its tests, it does not just retry. An observer reads the stack trace against a decision log and spins up a second agent, a Code Refactor Lobe, that rebuilds the failing module as a new cell in isolation. The cell is checked against a rules directory and a sandbox, and "if, and only if" the sandbox confirms full coverage, the observer grafts it into the live organism. The conclusion followed: the organism rewrites its own anatomy, and human maintenance, the most brittle part of engineering, is abstracted away.

That is a claim about a system that changes itself. It deserves the hardest check in this series, and the record has a good deal to say, because this is the one early idea that was actually exercised.

We split the note into ten claims and traced each. One is supported, four are softened, four are withdrawn and one is a metaphor. Filter the ledger and open the cards.

The sentences that fell are the ones that removed the person. The ones that survived describe a staged, reviewed, budgeted proposal.

02

The pipeline that exists

Six stages, each with a trail

Here is the real sequence. When a task keeps failing, the loop hands it to the evolution engine with the failure count and message. The engine states the missing capability, then asks a generator for a cell and a unit test, under limits that are enforced before the first call. The cell and its test run in a sandbox. A critic reviews the result. If everything holds, the engine writes a hashed, review-only proposal packet and stops.

What happens next belongs to the operator. Writing the cell into source, importing it and activating it are separate closed gates. The final one is a manifest entry that lists the cell with its hash. Without that entry the file is quarantined and never imported.

Each stage is described below. None of them is fast and none is autonomous, and each can say no.

The engine, critic and loader suites were counted in source. The Brain's test runner was not available in the environment used for this note, so none of them was run, and the figures from September and October are dated records.

As described, and as built

      04

      The record, in dates

      What the pipeline has actually done

      The pipeline has a short history, and one real run. It is worth reading carefully, because it is the whole evidence for the idea.

      01

      April 2026 · The note

      Described an observer that grafts a rebuilt cell into the live organism.

      02

      25 and 26 May 2026 · The only graft

      A task failed three times with "simulation plane capability missing". The generated cell passed the sandbox with a clean exit, and the operator integrated it on 26 May. It is an in-silico simulation cell, not a bug fix.

      03

      28 and 29 May 2026 · Guards go in

      Confined writes and the manifest quarantine are recorded as verified on 28 May. The critic's source is committed on 29 May.

      04

      7 September 2026 · Proposals get budgets

      Each proposal now runs under its own limits, enforced before generation. 26 tests.

      05

      16 October 2026 · The sandbox is repaired

      The container run learns to move generated code into writable temporary space while its root stays read-only.

      06

      10 November 2026 · Today

      One generated cell is active. No second graft appears in the record.

      One real proposal, one approval, one active cell. That is the honest scale of neurogenesis on the record today. It is a working process and a small result.

      05

      What nearly went wrong

      Four lessons the pipeline carries

      Behind the pipeline's caution sits a history of nearly going wrong. The Brain's own archive and charters record four lessons. They are the reason the note's automatic graft was never built.

      None of these is a triumph. Each is a repair made after the design had already failed on paper or in practice, and they are the reason the approval gate sits where it does.

      06

      What this note does not claim

      Boundary
      • It does not claim any code heals itself. The pipeline proposes and a person decides.
      • It does not claim cells are tested to full coverage. Coverage is not measured.
      • It does not claim the one grafted cell fixed a bug. It added a simulation capability that was missing.
      • It does not claim a second graft exists, or that generated cells improve the system.
      • It does not claim the engine reads the decision log or a rules directory. It does neither.
      • It does not claim the hosted repository is active. It is catalogued as dormant, and the local copy's last attended sessions are dated in the record.

      What it does claim is narrow and checkable: a staged, budgeted, critic-reviewed and operator-approved path exists for generated code, and one cell has gone through it.
