TRANSMISSION 002

What We Learned Deploying
Agents On-Chain

An April 2026 note described an immune system, a dead man's switch and a three-cortex vote guarding an agent that acts on chain. Checked against the record: the risk gate is real, tested and paper-only; no vote exists in the code we read; the dead-man reader watches organs, not trades; and nothing that can move value is allowed to live on this machine.

November 2026·Revised note·Custody·10 min read
Dated. This note describes the code and rulings as they stand on 10 November 2026. The note it replaces was published in April 2026 and described the design as if it were built.

Revised 10 November 2026

Rewritten to the Transmission standard. Nine claims were checked. Five are withdrawn, one is softened, and the page now describes what exists: a paper harness with two layers of risk control, a gated signing path that defaults to dry run, a heartbeat reader, and a custody rule that keeps assets out of reach of this machine.

Correction, 10 November 2026

The April note stated as built: a three-way vote with a two-of-three majority, an immune system that watches drawdown velocity and pattern deviation, and a dead man's switch that revokes keys and suspends the organism. None of those exist in the Brain code we read; the separately deployed organism's code was not opened. The white paper has one sentence saying agents vote on trades and another describing a fallback between two providers, but no voting module exists; the risk module checks stop-loss, take-profit, breakeven, frequency and cost; and the only dead-man mechanism built reads organ heartbeats and raises alarms. Those sentences are withdrawn.

01

Nine claims from April, checked

A ledger of what the old note said

The April note opened with a good observation. When an agent drafts an email, failure is cheap. When an agent acts on a ledger, a hallucination is catastrophic and the chain has no undo. Everything after that sentence was a design, and the note wrote it in the present tense: an immune system that monitors drawdown velocity, pattern deviation and protocol sanity; a dead man's switch that revokes keys; a vote among three cortexes.

We split the note into nine claims and traced each to a record. Five are withdrawn, because nothing in the code or the rulings supports them. One is softened, because the truth is narrower than the sentence. Two are supported. One design idea survived. Filter the ledger by verdict and open any card to see the reason.

One piece of context shapes everything below. The record describes two organisms with similar names. One was deployed separately and held a small real wallet; the other is the paper-only code in the Brain and a newer paper harness on this machine. The audit of 17 September 2026 says not to read one as evidence about the other, and nobody opened the live one to write this note.

The pattern is consistent. The sentences that described machinery were the ones that fell. The sentence about caution, and the idea of building walls around the model instead of improving the model, are the ones that stood.

02

What actually guards the money

Five pieces, each with a trail

The guards that exist are plainer than the ones the April note described, and they are tested. The first layer is deterministic: absolute dollar limits for each intent, each day and each position, a cap on open positions, a drawdown line past which no new risk is taken, and a cash floor. The limits are absolute amounts rather than percentages, because the custody rules say so.

The second layer is a local model used as a reviewer. It answers in strict JSON with a verdict of accept, shrink or veto and a size factor clamped between zero and one, so it can only ever make an intent smaller. It is resident-only: it asks which model is already loaded and never triggers a load. If it errors or times out, it is treated as absent, and when a reviewer is required, new risk is refused while risk-reducing sales still pass. A cap that stops you unwinding would be the false death of July in a new coat.

Around both sits the ledger. Every action is written to a hash-chained ledger before it happens, and the tests try to truncate, alter, forge and reorder it. In the Brain, the signing path for the Monad executor defaults to dry run and refuses unless the approval engine says otherwise, and generated code cannot import it.

Everything above is paper. The harness holds a simulated book seeded with a hundred dollars; its tests ran with no network. The 76 harness tests and 48 dead-man tests were re-run for this note on 10 November.

03

Walking one intent through the gate

As described, and as built

The April note described a path with three steps: three cortexes evaluate, two of three agree, value moves. That path does not exist. The path that does exist has four steps and no fifth: an intent is proposed, the caps judge it, a reviewer may shrink or veto it, and a paper broker records a simulated fill.

Choose a situation in the model and read both columns. The left column is marked not built throughout, because it is. The right column shows where each kind of bad intent stops. The point is not that the right column is clever. It is that it is small enough to test, and that its last step cannot send anything anywhere.

      The record, in dates

      How the organism got here

      The lessons in this note came from a messy history, and the history is worth keeping straight. The figures below come from the 17 September 2026 audit of the organism's origins and from the rulings it cites. They are dated, and they describe the past.

      01

      February 2026 · A simulation

      The organism runs as a simulation. Its early record is hundreds of cycles that all decide to wait.

      02

      29 May 2026 · The audit

      The operator asks why it is not growing. The finding: it has never traded on chain, every recorded cycle is a simulation, and its portfolio figure was a constant.

      03

      4 July 2026 · Live, on order

      The operator orders live trading on a separately deployed organism, with a 5 percent position cap and a cost shield.

      04

      6 July 2026 · The false death

      A blank balance reading made the organism shut itself down while its funds were untouched. The fix: three consecutive trusted low readings before death.

      05

      22 August to 17 September 2026 · The law

      A council vote sets the treasury doctrine: receive-only, with no signing key on the machine. The stage ruling of 17 September names the organism not eligible for acts.

      06

      17 September 2026 · A paper harness

      A paper harness is built under the custody rules: 49 tests at landing, 76 in its main suite when re-run on 10 November, no network in tests, and a ledger that proves it was not rewritten.

      The 6 July entry is the one that bears most on a dead man's switch. A switch that fires on one bad reading is a way to kill a healthy organism. Its fix was not a vote. It was three confirmations before death.

      06

      What this note does not claim

      Boundary
      • It does not claim any agent trades profitably. No profit-and-loss exists in the record.
      • It does not claim a vote, a revocation switch or a drawdown-velocity monitor exists. None is in the code we read; the separately deployed organism's code was not opened.
      • It does not claim the paper harness predicts live behaviour. It is a harness for proving a strategy or disproving it at no cost.
      • It does not claim the separately deployed organism is safe or unsafe. Nobody opened it to write this note, and the rules keep that boundary even for reading.
      • It does not claim the heartbeat reader's scheduled task is healthy today. On 10 November its runs ended with a non-zero exit, which is by design when it finds an organ in alarm, and a 10 November diagnosis found one missed run caused by a machine reboot. The alarms it lists were real; converting its task so it runs unattended waits on the operator.
      • It does not give figures for any wallet, address or position.

      What it does claim is narrow: a risk gate with two layers exists and passes its tests, signing is closed by default, and the rest of the April note was a design.
