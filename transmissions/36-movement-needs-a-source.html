TRANSMISSION 036

Movement Needs
a Source

The glass infinity at the top of this site's homepage now moves through a short cinematic passage, frame by frame, but only while a visitor scrolls it. This field note covers where that motion comes from, what it is allowed to cost a phone, every condition that stops it, and why a verified interface release is not evidence that Genesis is ready.

November 2026·Field note·Interface + Evidence·11 min read

Dated note. The assessment described here was made on 22 October 2026, when the motion work was still a local candidate. This page was written and published on 8 November 2026. It fills the place held under number 36 and is published after Transmission 37, which stays the latest in the sequence. Operational facts below describe 22 October, not today.

01

The source is the visitor

Native scroll after a real gesture · no clock · no autoplay

A loop that animates on its own timer proves only that a timer exists. The homepage hero is built the other way round. Its frame renderer has no animation clock, no autoplay and no list of frames to fetch ahead of time, and a repository test fails if a timer, an animation-frame loop, a video element or any use of the device pixel ratio ever appears in it. Scroll position is the only thing that selects a frame.

Not every scroll is the visitor's. A scroll counts only when a wheel turn, a touch drag, a pointer press or a scrolling key (the arrows, Page Up, Page Down, Home, End, Space) happened within the previous second. A click with no scroll selects nothing. A resize clears any recent gesture, so a layout change, or a position the browser restores on its own, leaves the image still. Intent observed while the artwork is off screen cannot arm it later.

The passage spans the first seventy percent of the hero's height. The frame shown is the scroll progress spread across the sequence and rounded to the nearest frame. Scroll back into the first one percent of the passage and the renderer lets go of everything: cached frames are released and the original still image returns. Pick an input below to see what the shipped rule does with it.

Wheel, then scroll

Selects a frame

The wheel marks a gesture; the scroll that follows within one second carries it. If the artwork is on screen and motion is allowed, the renderer asks for the one frame that matches the new position.

Shipped input routing on the homepage, pinned by a test that replays wheel, scroll and key events.

Touch drag

Selects a frame

On a phone the drag is the gesture. The same one-second window applies, and the phone rendition is the one requested.

Touch movement is one of the four gesture sources the input routing listens for.

Scrolling key

Selects a frame

Arrow Up, Arrow Down, Page Up, Page Down, Home, End and Space are gestures. Keyboard scrolling is eligible on exactly the same terms as a wheel. Other keys are not gestures for this purpose.

The test presses Page Down and confirms the following scroll counts.

Pointer press, then scroll

Selects a frame

Dragging a scrollbar begins with a pointer press. The scroll that follows within a second counts as the visitor's.

A pointer press records a gesture; it does not, by itself, move anything.

A click, no scroll

Selects nothing

A press without a scroll leaves the image where it is. The renderer is consulted only when the page actually scrolls.

Test assertion: a click alone cannot select cinematic frames.

A gesture over a second old

Selects nothing

A gesture is a permission with a one-second shelf life. After that, a scroll with no fresh input in front of it leaves the image still.

Test assertion: an old gesture cannot authorize later scrolling.

Resize or rotation

Selects nothing

Resizing clears any recent gesture; it recalculates layout and nothing more. If the new size crosses the line between the phone and desktop renditions, every decoded frame is released and the other rendition waits for a fresh gesture.

Tests: layout refresh cannot inherit click intent; a breakpoint resize releases old frames and cannot load the new rendition without intent.

A restored scroll position

Selects nothing

Returning to the page at a remembered position is not a gesture. With no input in the previous second, the image stays still wherever the page lands.

The input routing keeps intent separate from layout refreshes and restored scroll positions.

Gesture with the art off screen

Selects nothing

Intent observed while the artwork is out of view is discarded. It cannot arm a later sample once the art scrolls back into view; that needs a new gesture.

Test assertion: offscreen intent cannot arm a future visible sample.

02

A budget a phone can afford

Two renditions · encoded bytes measured per file · decoded memory estimated

The frames were sampled, at two sizes, from a single five-second AI-generated animation of the existing glass sculpture. The still image remains the first thing drawn and the permanent fallback; the canvas appears only after its first frame has decoded.

The phone rendition is chosen whenever the viewport is 700 pixels wide or less, the device reports 4 GB of memory or less, or the browser does not report its network at all. An unknown network is treated as slow, not fast. Before any frame is requested, the manifest is checked against both budgets. A manifest that exceeds a frame count, a dimension or a byte cap, or that points anywhere but this site, never fetches a frame.

Encoded totals are checked by a repository test against every shipped frame file. Decoded figures are width × height × 4 bytes for each kept frame: an estimate of pixel payload only, excluding encoded data, the canvas's own buffer and browser overhead.

One worker fetches and decodes, on both renditions. The design allowed the desktop two decodes at once; the shipped renderer uses one. Room is made in the cache before a decode starts, so the kept frames plus the one being decoded stay within the cap. The oldest frame is evicted and closed first, and scrolling back reuses a recently decoded frame without a new request. If the scroll moves on while a frame is downloading, the stale result is dropped before it is ever decoded.

03

Where motion stops

The still image is the resting state · motion has to keep qualifying

The still is not a fallback bolted on afterwards. It is the resting state, and every path out of motion leads back to it. The sequence below is the whole life of one frame. Open any stop condition beneath it to see what the renderer does.

01

Gesture

A wheel turn, touch drag, pointer press or scrolling key, then a scroll within one second.

02

Eligibility

The artwork is on screen, the tab is visible, motion is neither reduced nor paused, no sheet or foreground animation is open, and the connection is not marked Save-Data, 2g or slow-2g.

03

Manifest

Read once from this site, without credentials, and checked against both budgets before it is trusted.

04

Nearest frame

One request, for the frame the scroll points at now. A frame the scroll has already moved past is dropped before decoding.

05

Decode

Room is made first. A decode that finishes late, for a target the visitor has already left, is closed and never painted.

06

Paint

The canvas is shown only once a frame is ready; until then the still stays. The canvas is hidden from assistive technology, and the still keeps its written description.

Reduced motion is on

No request is made. The still stays, and the pause control reports reduced motion.

Test: reduced motion makes zero requests.

The visitor pressed Pause motion

No request is made. Anything in flight is cancelled and every decoded frame is released.

Test: denied motion makes zero requests.

Save-Data, 2g or slow-2g

No request is made. The connection's own signal wins over the art.

Test: Save-Data, 2g and slow-2g make zero requests.

Tab hidden, page left, art scrolled away

The request in flight is aborted, every decoded frame is released, the canvas shrinks to a single pixel, and the still returns.

Test: offscreen, hidden, pagehide and policy changes immediately release and restore the poster.

A fetch, decode or draw fails

The renderer stops once, for the rest of that page view, and leaves the still in place. There is no retry loop.

Test: failures fail once to the poster without a retry loop.

No canvas, no decoder, no JavaScript

Nothing is attached. The responsive still image, with its description, is the whole hero.

The renderer checks for canvas, image decoding and request cancellation before it attaches anything.

A sheet opens or another animation runs

The sequence brakes, so only one thing owns motion on the page at a time.

The homepage arbiter allows one finite foreground animation owner.

Scrolled back to the top

Back inside the first one percent of the passage, everything is released and the original still is what the visitor sees.

The renderer brakes below one percent of progress.

Motion here is evidence of exactly one thing: that a visitor moved.
04

A verified release is not a ready organism

Evidence boundary · the record of 22 October 2026

On 22 October the motion work was prepared as a local candidate: one authorized generated animation, adaptive scroll frames, a mobile layout and finite rendering, with 382 repository tests and 80 browser checks passing. Nothing was pushed or published that day. That is real evidence, and it is evidence about an interface.

Its own research set the limits of that evidence in writing. Passing unit tests imply no performance or accessibility certification. Real-device and production network behaviour stay unverified until they are separately measured. The review sampled five frames of the animation; it was not a frame-by-frame certification, and no physical-device Safari or battery measurement was made. The loading targets (largest paint within 2.5 seconds, layout shift of at most 0.1, interaction response within 200 milliseconds) are acceptance targets, not results.

The same evening's record about Genesis reads differently. A guard service reported healthy. Autonomous building was held: the build loop stayed parked behind the operator's own stop instruction. The heartbeat organ had been refusing to beat earlier that evening, because it could not read the ledger head, and it was then paused ahead of a recovery procedure that only the operator performs by hand. Agents prepared that procedure and executed none of it. When the day's record closes, recovery is still pending and unproven.

None of that contradicts the interface evidence, and none of the interface evidence answers it. A green test suite describes what it tests. The animated hero can show that a visitor scrolled. It cannot show that the organism behind the site is building or has recovered, and on 22 October the record showed neither. Filter the claims below by what the record supports.

Supported

The hero moves only in response to a visitor's scroll.

The renderer has no clock or autoplay, and the input routing requires a gesture within one second before a scroll. Both are pinned by repository tests.

Supported

The shipped frames fit their encoded budgets.

A test reads every shipped frame, checks its dimensions against the manifest and sums the bytes: 614,186 of 1,048,576 on phones, 1,814,132 of 2,097,152 on desktop.

Supported

Reduced motion, Pause, Save-Data and slow connections request no frames.

Each of those states is a test case that asserts zero requests and a hidden canvas.

Estimate

Decoded frames never exceed about 4.2 MiB on phones.

That is the pixel payload of four kept frames at 640 × 426. Encoded data, the canvas buffer and browser overhead are not included, and total memory was not measured.

Not measured

It loads quickly and responds smoothly on real phones.

No physical-device or production network measurement is in the record. The loading targets are acceptance targets, not results.

Not certified

Every frame of the animation is clean.

Five sampled frames were reviewed for shape, colour and continuity. That is a sample, not a frame-by-frame review.

Not supported

Genesis was operationally ready on 22 October.

Building was held, the heartbeat was paused, and recovery was pending. The record supports none of the readiness this claim implies.

Not supported

Genesis was building on its own.

The record shows the build loop parked behind the operator's own stop instruction.

Not supported

382 passing tests mean the system works.

They mean the website candidate passed the checks it was given. They say nothing about the organism's recovery.

What this note does not claim

It does not describe Genesis today. Everything operational on this page is the record of 22 October 2026, written down on 8 November without being updated or rounded up.

It does not certify the hero's performance, its accessibility or its behaviour on any particular phone. It reports what the shipped files and their tests pin down, and it labels the rest as estimated, not measured or not supported.

05

Movement needs a source. So does a claim.

Closing transmission

The rule that governs the hero is short. Nothing moves unless the visitor moved it, nothing loads that the budget cannot pay for, and every failure falls back to the still image.

The rule for this note has the same shape. Every measurement on the page comes from a shipped file or a dated record, every limit sits next to the number it limits, and what was not yet true on 22 October is written down as not yet true. An interface can be finished while the organism behind it is still recovering. Saying both, on the same page and under the same date, is the point.
