Public Proof · Live from chain

The organism tells you it is alive. Or it doesn't.

Every thirty minutes each organism in the Unwind ecosystem writes a heartbeat to a canister on the Internet Computer. Your browser is reading that canister directly — there is no Unwind server between you and this data, and nothing on this page can be edited by the systems it reports on.

What this page is: an attestation of liveness, plus the attested head of the Genesis gate's internal ledger — its sequence number and a truncated hash, carried in the heartbeat note.

What it is not: the ledger contents. The gate's records stay on the machine that wrote them. What travels here is a fingerprint of where that ledger stood at the last beat.

The precise claim: "the head was X as of the last beat" — at thirty-minute granularity. Not continuous attestation. At any moment up to half an hour of writes may exist that this page has not yet seen. That limit is written into the governing ruling, and it is stated here for the same reason.

What a stranger can verify here

  • Which organisms are beating, and which have gone quiet — including ones we would rather were green.
  • The Genesis gate's ledger head at the last beat, written with a credential the gate itself never holds.
  • That the attestation is carried by a third party — an Internet Computer canister — not by the machine making the claim.
  • That this page cannot be quietly corrected: it reads the canister in your browser, live.

What it deliberately does not claim

  • It is not tamper-proof within a beat. Up to thirty minutes of ledger writes may be unwitnessed at any instant.
  • It does not publish ledger contents — only the head. Records stay on the machine that wrote them.
  • A green light means a heartbeat arrived. It does not mean the work behind it was correct.
  • A stale organism is not necessarily broken — but we show it stale rather than hide it, because a cron that runs and fails looks exactly like one that works.

Don't take our word for it

The canister is public and permits cross-origin reads. Run this yourself, from your machine, with no credential and nothing of ours in the path. If your output disagrees with what is rendered above, the canister is right and this page is wrong.
